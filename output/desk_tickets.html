Reflection

Written after the clean run. Based only on what actually happened (see the ticket Actual sections and the Cash tab).

Overall, I think the agents performed well, but the runs also showed where having multiple agents can add unnecessary complexity.

Ticket 101

The team found the stock problem, the unpaid vendor invoice, got the payment approved, and drafted the customer response correctly. The main difference from what I expected was that it also created an extra $8 purchase order for the tee. I originally thought paying invoice 501 would already release the reprint, but the database did not fully confirm that, so the agent took the safer route.

Ticket 102

This was the cleanest run and was almost exactly what I expected: Boss → Facilities → Accounting, then I approved the $2,400 rent payment. The only unnecessary step was that Facilities checked cash and Accounting checked it again.

Ticket 103

I think the team handled this one well because it did not just mark the order as impossible. It recognized that 8 hoodies were available, that restocking the other 12 would cost $264 when only $152 of cash remained, and the Boss offered $55 each with partial fulfillment as an option. No payment was made and the customer message was only drafted.

What I would add: since we don't have the money to restock the other 12 hoodies, we could ask the Yale AI Club to prepay those 12 (12 × $55 = $660). That covers the $264 restock and lets us order immediately. With Bulldog Print Co's 5-day lead time, they would ship about 5 days after the prepayment (around 2026-09-05 if paid and approved on 8/31). That way the order isn't left on hold: we're actively working on it, funded with the client's money instead of cash we don't have. We couldn't do this in this homework because no revenue comes in, so the agents correctly didn't count any customer money. In a real shop, the Boss could offer prepayment in the customer draft, and Accounting would request the PO once the prepayment arrives, still with human approval.

Cash controls

The cash controls also worked well. We started with $3,400, paid $848 for Ticket 101 and $2,400 for Ticket 102, and finished with $152, which matches the database.

Ticket status vs. real outcome

One issue I noticed is that the ticket status and the real outcome can get out of sync after human approvals. Tickets 101 and 102 still say waiting_on_approval in the database because that was the Boss's last update before I approved the requests, even though the approvals were completed afterward. The dashboard works around this by showing "Approved by you," but I think the cleaner design would be for the approval flow to update the ticket once its final pending approval is completed.

fixed going forward The approval flow now marks a ticket resolved when its last pending request is approved, with the note "All approvals granted by [approver]". This only applies when the Boss left the ticket waiting_on_approval; a ticket waiting on a customer reply, like 103, keeps its status. The clean run above was not changed and still shows the original behavior.

Where one agent + tools would have been simpler

One part that probably would have been simpler with one agent + tools is Ticket 102. Checking a lease, checking cash, and requesting the rent payment is pretty linear, so having Facilities and Accounting hand work back and forth added some overhead without much extra reasoning.

What the team could handle with today's tools

  1. Another out-of-stock customer order and decide whether it can be restocked.
  2. Another upcoming rent or unpaid vendor invoice and prepare the required approval.
  3. Another bulk-order discount request by checking stock, costs, margins, and cash.

What it could not solve today

  1. Tracking where a vendor shipment physically is — we would need a vendor/carrier tracking tool.
  2. Handling payroll or employee issues — we would need payroll/HR data and probably an HR agent.
  3. Deciding which marketing campaign is performing best — we would need sales/marketing analytics tools and probably a Marketing agent.
